What Is F1?

THE PINNACLE OF MOTOR RACING

Formula One — officially the FIA Formula One World Championship — is the highest class of international single-seater auto racing sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). Founded in 1950, it stands as the world's most prestigious and technologically advanced motorsport series.

Each season, ten constructor teams field two drivers apiece, competing across a calendar of 24 Grand Prix events held on circuits spanning six continents. The cars themselves are engineering masterpieces: carbon-fibre monocoques producing over 1,000 brake horsepower from turbocharged 1.6-litre V6 hybrid power units, capable of reaching 0–100 km/h in under 2.5 seconds and braking from 300 km/h to a standstill in fewer than 70 metres.

Strategy is equally decisive — tyre management, pit-stop timing, weather reading, and split-second decision-making separate champions from the rest. Drivers accumulate points across the season towards both the Drivers' Championship and the Constructors' Championship, making every lap a complex chess match played at breathtaking velocity.

HOW F1 WORKS

01

The Car

F1 cars weigh just 798 kg including driver. Their aerodynamic bodies generate up to 3.5 G of downforce in high-speed corners, pressing the car into the asphalt for superior grip and stability — literally more force than a fighter jet.

02

Power Unit

The 1.6L V6 turbo-hybrid engine combines an internal combustion engine with energy recovery systems (ERS-K and ERS-H), harvesting energy from braking and exhaust heat to deliver combined outputs exceeding 1,000 bhp — with extraordinary fuel efficiency.

03

Tyres & Strategy

Pirelli supplies five tyre compounds per race weekend. Teams gamble on one-stop or multi-stop strategies, balancing raw pace against tyre degradation. A perfectly timed pit stop lasting under 2.5 seconds can gain or lose a race-winning position.

04

Qualifying

A three-part knockout system (Q1, Q2, Q3) determines grid positions. Drivers have one hot lap to post the fastest time. A single flying lap under immense pressure decides whether you start from pole or at the back of the pack.

05

Points System

Points are awarded from P1 to P10 on a 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 scale, with an extra point for the fastest lap if the driver finishes in the top ten. Sprint races award additional points. Consistency across 24 rounds crowns a champion.

06

DRS

The Drag Reduction System allows a pursuing driver within one second of the car ahead to open a flap in their rear wing, reducing aerodynamic drag by up to 15% and adding ~12 km/h. Activated in designated zones, it transforms overtaking opportunities.

THE TEAMS

Red Bull Racing
Milton Keynes, UK

The most dominant team of the hybrid era. Powered by Honda RBPT, they claimed a record-breaking 21 wins in 2023, led by four-time World Champion Max Verstappen.

McLaren
Woking, UK

The iconic British team famous for Senna and Prost. McLaren surged back to the front in 2024 with Norris and Piastri delivering thrilling performances on Pirelli and Mercedes power.

Ferrari
Maranello, Italy

The most storied team in F1 history. La Scuderia carries 16 Constructors' Championships and the passion of the Tifosi worldwide — always a title contender with their own power unit.

Mercedes-AMG
Brackley, UK

Eight consecutive Constructors' Championships from 2014–2021 made Mercedes the benchmark of the hybrid era. With Lewis Hamilton now at Ferrari, the Silver Arrows rebuild under George Russell.

Aston Martin
Silverstone, UK

Bankrolled by billionaire Lawrence Stroll and guided by two-time champion Fernando Alonso, Aston Martin's striking green AMR24 and vast new factory signal serious future intentions.

Alpine
Enstone, UK

The French works team — formerly Renault — competes with their own in-house power unit. A team steeped in history including Alonso's back-to-back titles in 2005 and 2006.

Williams
Grove, UK

Seven Constructors' Championships make Williams one of F1's most successful teams. Now under Dorilton Capital ownership, they use Mercedes power and are resurgent with young talent.

RB (AlphaTauri)
Faenza, Italy

Red Bull's second team rebranded as Visa Cash App RB. A vital proving ground for Red Bull junior drivers on their path to the senior squad, running Honda RBPT engines.

Kick Sauber
Hinwil, Switzerland

Transitioning into the Audi works team from 2026, the Hinwil-based outfit is building toward a bold new chapter as one of Europe's premier automotive manufacturers enters F1.

Haas
Kannapolis, USA

The only American-owned F1 constructor, running Ferrari power. Haas operates one of F1's leanest outfits while consistently punching above its weight in a brutally competitive midfield.
